Distinguishes woody Prosopis regrowth from genuine grassland recovery after clearing, using a 12-month NDVI time series.
How it works. Prosopis is evergreen: it holds a green, moist canopy through the dry season, so its NDVI has a high dry-season floor and low seasonal amplitude. Grassland does the opposite — it greens sharply in the rains and senesces, giving a low floor and high amplitude. Each site is scored on those two numbers.
Scores re-invasion risk across the visible area from seed-source pressure, moisture, terrain and fire history.
Inputs. Seed-source pressure = distance to mapped Prosopis (the strongest documented predictor of spread). Moisture = Sentinel-2 NDMI. Terrain = slope from Copernicus 30 m DEM; flat alluvial ground carries higher risk. Fire = MODIS MCD64A1 burned area, which opens ground for colonisation.

Limitation. This is a weighted composite of proxies, not a calibrated probability. Weights are exposed so you can tune them against local knowledge.
Ranks where clearing delivers the greatest return for grazing, water access and erosion control.
Inputs. Grazing gain = area of low-woody, seasonally green land adjacent to the stand. Water access = proximity to surface water and drainage detected with NDWI. Erosion = slope from the DEM. Density = size of the mapped Prosopis cluster.
Verifies reported field activity against the satellite record — independent quality control for MRV claims.
How it works. For each record the tool takes the cleanest Sentinel-2 scene before and after the stated date, samples NDVI at the exact coordinate, and checks whether the drop matches the claim. A claim is verified only if the drop exceeds the threshold within the window.

Not verified means the satellite record does not support the claim — it does not by itself prove the work was not done. Cloud, scene gaps and small plot sizes all produce false negatives, so treat it as a screening flag for follow-up.
Method & caveats. Detection is a spectral indicator, not a trained classifier. It combines NDVI (B08/B04) with NDMI (B08/B11) from Sentinel-2 L2A. Other dense, moist woody species can give the same signal, so findings require field validation.

Data: Microsoft Planetary Computer — Sentinel-2 L2A, Copernicus DEM GLO-30, MODIS MCD64A1. Place names from OpenStreetMap.
